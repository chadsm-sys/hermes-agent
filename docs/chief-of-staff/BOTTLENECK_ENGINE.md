# BOTTLENECK_ENGINE — One Constraint at a Time

Theory of constraints, applied to a life and a business: at any moment there
is **one** binding constraint that governs total throughput. Working anywhere
else is motion, not progress.

## Principle

Every system — a revenue pipeline, a training program, an AI gateway — has a
single slowest stage. Improving any non-bottleneck stage produces inventory,
not output. The Bottleneck Engine exists to answer one question every
morning, with evidence:

> **"What single constraint, if relaxed today, would raise the value of
> everything else?"**

The Morning Plan carries exactly one **Biggest Bottleneck**. Not a top five.
The discipline of naming one is the point: a list of five bottlenecks is a
confession that the analysis hasn't finished.

## How a Bottleneck Is Identified

1. **Measure flow, not effort.** The engine looks for where value stalls —
   revenue blocked on a credential, a launch blocked on a review, recovery
   blocked on sleep — not where the most activity happens. Busy stages are
   rarely the constraint; starved and blocked stages are.
2. **Demand evidence.** A bottleneck claim needs the same evidence chain as
   any recommendation ([DECISION_ENGINE.md](DECISION_ENGINE.md)): the
   observation that shows the stall, and the estimate of what relaxing it is
   worth.
3. **Check the domain boundary.** Bottlenecks are searched across *all*
   domains, because the binding constraint of a business is frequently not in
   the business (sleep constrains judgment; an expiring credential constrains
   revenue; a family commitment constrains the calendar). A single-domain
   bottleneck search is a category error.
4. **Distinguish constraint from condition.** A hard constraint the human has
   chosen (evenings are family time; the attention window is 90 minutes) is
   **physics, not a bottleneck** — the engine designs around it and never
   nominates it for "fixing."

## How a Bottleneck Is Attacked

In order of preference:

1. **Exploit** — get more out of the constraint without spending anything:
   reorder work so the constraint is never idle and never works on waste.
2. **Subordinate** — make every other stage serve the constraint's schedule,
   even if those stages become locally "inefficient."
3. **Elevate** — only after exploit and subordinate are exhausted: spend
   money or build capacity to relax the constraint itself.
4. **Repeat** — when a constraint breaks, the engine *must* re-run the
   search. Yesterday's bottleneck is the most dangerous lie in the system,
   because it still feels true.

## Interaction with the Rest of the Layer

- **Morning Plan** — the Biggest Bottleneck headlines the brief; the
  Executive Recommendation is usually (not always) the highest-ranked attack
  on it.
- **Decision Engine** — bottleneck-relaxing work naturally scores high on
  strategic alignment; the ranking contract stays unchanged. The bottleneck
  informs estimates; it never bypasses the ranker.
- **Compound Engine** — recurring bottlenecks are asset signals. If the same
  class of constraint appears three times, the correct elevation is an asset
  (a playbook, an automation, a contract), not a third manual fix
  ([COMPOUND_ENGINE.md](COMPOUND_ENGINE.md)).
- **Evening Report** — reports whether the named bottleneck moved, with the
  measurement. "We worked hard near it" is not movement.

## Failure Modes to Refuse

- **Bottleneck churn** — naming a new constraint daily without evidence the
  old one broke. Constraints usually take days to relax; the engine must
  have the spine to repeat itself.
- **Comfortable bottlenecks** — nominating constraints that are pleasant to
  work on over the true constraint that is tedious or scary.
- **Fixing conditions** — attempting to optimize the human's chosen life
  constraints instead of the systems inside them.
